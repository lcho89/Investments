# Company Categorisation and Sell Discipline

Every holding gets exactly one primary category. The category decides which metrics
matter and which sell triggers apply, so getting it wrong makes the rest of the analysis
answer the wrong question. State the category and defend it in one sentence. If a name
straddles two, name both and say which governs.

Record the category in the thesis file. Changing it is a material event and needs the
same justification as a rating change.

---

## 1. Slow growers — owned for the dividend

Analyse: payout ratio (dividends ÷ earnings) and whether it is high or low for the
sector. Confirm FCF covers the dividend.

**Sell when:** market share lost two years running · no new products and R&D curtailed ·
two recent acquisitions that look like diworsification · balance sheet deteriorated
paying for acquisitions · yield no longer competitive.

## 2. Stalwarts — large, unlikely to fail

Analyse: P/E is the key metric. Check diversification efforts (M&A, new products) and
whether they have traction or will dilute earnings. Long-term growth rate versus
guidance and analyst expectations — has momentum held? How did it behave in the last
recession?

**Sell when:** P/E far outside its normal range · new products delivering mixed results ·
trading at P/E 15 while equal-quality peers sit at 11–12 · no officer or director has
bought shares · a division contributing ~25% of earnings is exposed to a downturn ·
growth rate decelerating.

## 3. Cyclicals

Analyse: inventory levels — turnover = COGS ÷ average inventory — and % change in
inventory per year. Then place the company in the cycle: early, middle, or peak. Say
what evidence puts it there.

**Sell when:** costs rising or plants at full capacity · commodity prices falling ·
inventories building · competition intensifying · labour conflict · demand slowing ·
capex doubling to build new plants rather than modernise old ones · costs cut but still
uncompetitive against imports.

For commodity and miner positions, the underlying commodity price *is* the cycle
indicator — pair inventory data with spot and term prices, and fetch both.

## 4. Fast growers

Analyse: is the enriching product a major part of the business, or a rounding error?
Earnings and revenue growth in recent years is the key metric. Evidence of duplicating
success in new geographies or product lines. Room and capacity to keep growing.
Competitive differentiation. Is expansion accelerating or decelerating — quantify it
(e.g. three new products this year versus two last; new product growing 2× the core and
rising as a share of revenue).

**Sell when:** the second phase of growth is ending · P/E has outrun the growth rate ·
sales declining · senior people leaving for rivals · the story told to institutions has
become promotional rather than factual.

## 5. Turnarounds

Analyse: net cash position — survival depends on it. Net interest coverage (EBIT ÷
interest expense). Debt structure and how long it can operate in the red. If already
distressed, what the path back looks like. Then the plan itself: which divisions are
profitable and which are not, with revenue breakdown and per-unit profit or loss.
Evaluate management's plan — closures, asset sales, cutting operating losses, recovery.

**Sell when:** it has turned and is behaving like its old self again · debt rising ·
inventories building · P/E high relative to earnings prospects · the strongest division
depends on a single customer.

## 6. Asset plays

Analyse: value of the assets, and specifically whether hidden assets — IP, property,
inventory, plant — are carried below worth. Current long-term debt and whether more is
being taken on.

**Sell when:** the acquirer has shown up · shares are being issued to finance operations
· divisions sold cheaply · tax-rate cuts erode loss carryforwards · institutional
ownership rises materially.

---

## Applying this

In every thesis, include:

```
## Category
<one of the six> — <one-sentence defence>

## Category Analysis
<the metrics that category requires, with figures and sources>

## Sell Triggers
<the category's triggers, each marked: not triggered / watch / TRIGGERED, with the
observable that would flip it, and today's reading>
```

The sell triggers are the falsification test. A thesis with no triggered-condition list
cannot be wrong, which means it cannot be right either. Review them every cycle and
report the reading, even when nothing has moved — "no triggers active" is a finding.
